🔒 Part 2: Procurement Data Rules

▼
TierExamples in this roomOn a phone AI app?
TIER 1 · PUBLICWhat Fe500D means, how to calculate bar weight, generic specs, how a comparative statement is structured, maintenance conceptsYes
TIER 2 · INTERNALIndent formats, consumption patterns, a challan with vendor and site names removed, machine logs with IDs codedOnly after anonymising
TIER 3 · CONFIDENTIALReal quoted and negotiated rates, rate contracts, vendor terms, vendor bank details, the approved-vendor list with rates, project-wise material budgets, hire-contract ratesNever

Why rates are the most sensitive thing in this room

Suroj's negotiated rates tell a competitor what Suroj pays, which tells them what Suroj can bid. They tell a vendor what their competitor offered. A comparative statement with real rates, pasted into a free app, is Suroj's buying position sitting on someone else's server.

💡 How to practise on real work safely: rename vendors V1/V2/V3 and multiply every rate by the same factor (e.g. × 1.13). The ranking, the landed-cost logic and the percentage differences all survive. Convert back in your own Excel.

SUROJ SCENARIO The CS that went to the vendor

▼

Scenario

A buyer asks AI to draft a "counter-offer email to V2" and pastes the full comparative statement for context. The draft reads well. He copies it into Outlook and sends it. Paragraph two mentions that "another supplier has offered ₹55,900 ex-works with 4-day delivery".

Impact

V2 now knows V3's rate and delivery. V3's quotation, given in confidence, has been disclosed. Suroj's negotiating position has been handed over in one paragraph.

Rule

Give AI only what the email needs. Read every AI-drafted vendor email specifically for information that shouldn't be in it — AI uses whatever context you give it.

PROMPT A — COMPARATIVE STATEMENT
These are three supplier quotations (training dummies) for the same item. Build a comparative statement table with rows: basic rate, freight, landed rate per MT before GST, total for the quantity before GST, GST treatment, payment terms, delivery period, validity. Columns: V1, V2, V3. Show the calculation for every landed rate and total step by step. Rank the three on landed cost only. Do not recommend a vendor yet.
PROMPT B — THE REAL COMPARISON
Now factor in payment terms. Assume our cost of funds is 10% per year (training assumption). Treat V2's payment date as day 45 after delivery, V1's as day 30, and V3's as 50% paid 4 days before delivery and 50% on delivery. For each vendor, estimate the interest cost of paying earlier than day 45, show the working, and give an "effective cost" total. Then list the non-price factors a buyer should weigh (delivery urgency for a raft pour, validity, vendor track record [confirm]) — without making the final decision.
Trainer answer key — reveal after tables report

Landed (before GST): V1 ₹57,700/MT → ₹11,54,000 · V2 ₹57,800/MT → ₹11,56,000 · V3 ₹55,900 + 1,650 = ₹57,550/MT → ₹11,51,000 (cheapest on rate)

With payment terms at 10% p.a. (pre-GST, approximate): V1 pays 15 days earlier than V2 → ≈ ₹4,740 → effective ≈ ₹11,58,740 · V3 pays 49 and 45 days earlier on two halves → ≈ ₹14,820 → effective ≈ ₹11,65,820 · V2 ₹11,56,000 → V2 is the cheapest real buy.

But: if the raft pour can't wait 10 days, V3's 4-day delivery may be worth the premium. That's the judgement AI should lay out and not make. Tables that recommended V3 on rate alone — that's exactly the error a CS exists to prevent.

🧪 Data Lab 1: Indents & POs — Above Contract, Over-Ordered, Split

▼
⏱ 18 minutes · File: 1_Indents_POs.csv · 154 POs · 6 sites · led by Purchase

The situation

The PO register is the first thing audit samples. Rate contracts exist for steel, cement, binding wire and RMC — did buyers stick to them? Did anyone order more than site asked for? And did anyone break an order in two to keep it below the ₹5 lakh line that needs the Head of Procurement's signature?

📄 1_Indents_POs.csv

📱 How your table runs it

  1. Upload the CSV. Send Prompt 1 — profile first.
  2. Send Prompt 2 — the seven checks. Scribe records counts.
  3. Challenger sends Prompt 3 — the split-PO hunt. It's the one AI misses without being told how to look.
  4. Checker proves two findings.
  5. Writer sends Prompt 4.
PROMPT 1 — PROFILE
The attached file is a SYNTHETIC indent and PO register of an Indian construction company. How many POs, total PO value, and value by Site and by Material? How many POs are above ₹5,00,000? Show your working. Don't look for problems yet.
PROMPT 2 — SEVEN CHECKS
Check every PO: 1. PO_Rate above RC_Rate (where RC_Rate exists) 2. PO_Qty more than 5% above Indent_Qty 3. PO_Date before Indent_Date 4. PO_Date after Required_By 5. PO_Value above ₹5,00,000 but Approved_By is not Head of Procurement 6. PO_Value ≠ PO_Qty × PO_Rate (±₹1) 7. For check 1, the ₹ paid above contract (difference × PO_Qty) List PO_Nos with the values that prove each.
PROMPT 3 — THE SPLIT-PO HUNT
Look for pairs of POs with the same Site, Vendor_Code and Material, dated within 2 days of each other, each below ₹5,00,000 but together above it. List every pair with dates and values. Who approved them?
PROMPT 4 — THE NOTE UPWARD
Draft a 150-word note to the Head of Procurement: findings by check, count and ₹ value, and three process fixes (e.g. a system block on PO rate above RC). State facts only. For split POs, say they "match the pattern of an order split to stay under the approval limit" — don't assume intent.

✅ Prove it — before you score it

  • ▸ One RC breach: PO_Rate vs RC_Rate on the row. Multiply the difference by PO_Qty.
  • ▸ One split pair: search the vendor in the Field Kit Data tab. Two POs, same material, a day apart, both under ₹5 lakh?
  • ▸ One late PO: Required_By vs PO_Date — did site get it in time?

🧪 Data Lab 2: Quotations & CS — Did the Cheapest Vendor Win?

▼
⏱ 18 minutes · File: 2_Quotations_CS.csv · 151 quotes · 46 RFQs · led by Purchase

The situation

Every PO should trace back to at least three quotes and a comparative statement where L1 on landed rate wins — unless someone wrote down why not. Audit will recalculate the CS, not read it. And they'll notice something the buyers may not have: two vendor codes that share one GSTIN, and always quote a few percent apart.

📄 2_Quotations_CS.csv

📱 How your table runs it

  1. Upload the CSV. Send Prompt 1 — recalculate every CS.
  2. Send Prompt 2 — the award checks. Challenger watches for the two justified non-L1 awards — flagging them costs you the trap points.
  3. Send Prompt 3 — the GSTIN question.
  4. Writer sends Prompt 4.
PROMPT 1 — RECALCULATE THE CS
The attached file is SYNTHETIC quotation data. For every quote, recalculate landed rate = Basic_Rate + Freight_per_Unit. List any Quote_ID where the Landed_Rate in the file doesn't match. Then, for each RFQ, rank quotes on your recalculated landed rate.
PROMPT 2 — AWARD CHECKS
For each RFQ: 1. Is the Selected = Y vendor L1 on recalculated landed rate? If not, is there a Justification? 2. Does the RFQ have at least 3 quotes? 3. For the selected quote, is PO_Date on or before Valid_Until? Separate "not L1 with justification" from "not L1 without justification" — they are not the same finding.
PROMPT 3 — WHO ARE THESE VENDORS?
Group all quotes by Vendor_GSTIN. Does any GSTIN appear under more than one Vendor_Code? If yes: which RFQs did both quote in, who won each time, how far apart were their rates (in %), and were their payment and delivery terms the same?
PROMPT 4 — THE VERIFICATION MEMO
Draft a factual memo to the Head of Procurement about the two vendor codes sharing one GSTIN. Include the table of RFQs and rates. Recommend: verify both registrations with the vendor-onboarding team, pause new RFQ invitations to both until verified, and review the four awards. Do not accuse anyone of collusion — describe the pattern and what it could indicate.

✅ Prove it — before you score it

  • ▸ One CS error: add Basic_Rate + Freight on the calculator.
  • ▸ One non-L1 award: sort that RFQ's quotes yourself. Is there a Justification?
  • ▸ The GSTIN pair: search the GSTIN in Data. Put the two codes' rates side by side for each shared RFQ — what's the % gap?
⚠️ Say this clearly: a shared GSTIN and a steady price gap are a pattern, not proof. What happens next — vendor verification, vigilance, legal — is a decision for procurement leadership. AI's job was to make the pattern visible.
