🧠 Part 1: What AI Is Good and Bad at in Accounts

▼

AI is a reader and a writer, not a calculator

A language model predicts the next word. That makes it very good at reading messy documents and writing clean output — and unreliable at arithmetic, because a total is just another string of digits it predicts. It will produce a wrong GST figure in exactly the same confident format as a right one.

Where AI earns its place in an Accounts workflow:

Read & extract
invoice, notice, statement
→
Structure
tables, checklists
→
Compute
you, in Excel / calculator
→
Explain & draft
emails, replies, notes

✅ Let AI do this

  • Pull line items, HSN, GSTIN, amounts out of a photographed invoice
  • Explain a notice or a circular in plain language
  • Turn a messy ledger dump into a clean, sorted table
  • Write Excel formulas from a plain-English description
  • List possible reasons two balances don't match
  • Draft vendor queries, payment follow-ups, reply skeletons

❌ Never trust AI with this alone

  • Final totals, tax amounts, TDS amounts, interest
  • The current GST rate on any item, or a section / rule number
  • Whether an ITC claim is eligible
  • Due dates for returns and payments
  • The final wording of anything filed with a tax authority
  • Anything with a real PAN, bank account or employee salary in it
THE RECOMPUTE RULE — ADD TO EVERY NUMBERS PROMPT
Do not calculate any new totals yourself. Copy every number exactly as it appears in the document. Where a total is printed, show it, and separately show the line items so I can recompute it. If any figure is unclear or cut off in the image, write [unreadable] instead of guessing. At the end, list every figure I should verify on a calculator.
💡 One sentence to remember: AI reads the invoice, you add it up. The two minutes you spend on the calculator are what make the output yours to sign.

🔒 Part 2: The Accounts Data Rules — Three Tiers

▼
TierExamples in Suroj AccountsOn a phone AI app?
TIER 1 · PUBLICGST law as published, CBIC circulars, Income Tax Act text, generic Excel questions, accounting concepts (retention, mobilisation advance, RA bills)Yes, freely
TIER 2 · INTERNALLedger structure, ageing patterns, reconciliation logic, format of an RA bill, a notice with names and numbers removedOnly after anonymising
TIER 3 · CONFIDENTIALReal invoices, bank statements, vendor bank details, PAN / GSTIN of real parties, salaries, payroll, project margins, client billing, loan and BG documents, audit findings, tax notices in original formNever — not even "just to format it"

The anonymisation method — 90 seconds, every time

  • Names → codes. Vendor A, Vendor B, Site 1, Site 2, Client X
  • Identifiers → removed. PAN, GSTIN, bank account, IFSC, invoice numbers, phone numbers
  • Amounts → scaled. Multiply or divide every amount by the same factor (e.g. ÷ 7). Differences, ratios and patterns survive; the real figures don't
  • Dates → shifted. Move every date by the same number of days if timing is sensitive
  • Keep the key on paper or in your own Excel. Never in the chat
💡 Why scaling works for reconciliation: if the ledger and the statement are both divided by 7, the difference is also divided by 7. The AI can still find which entries cause it. You multiply back afterwards.

GLOBAL Samsung — internal material pasted into a public chatbot

▼

What happened

In 2023, Samsung staff pasted internal source code and meeting notes into ChatGPT to debug and summarise. The material left the company's control.

Impact

Samsung restricted generative AI use internally. The cost was never quantified — with this kind of exposure you rarely find out what it cost you.

Lesson for Suroj Accounts

These were trained engineers doing something that felt like routine work. "Please format this bank statement" feels equally routine. The instinct to be efficient is exactly what causes the leak.

SUROJ SCENARIO The vendor bank change that looked like a formatting job

▼

Scenario

An accountant receives an email from a "vendor" asking to update bank details, with a scanned letter. To save time she photographs the letter and asks AI to extract the new account details into the vendor master format.

Two problems

First, a real vendor's bank details are now on a third-party server. Second — and worse — AI did the extraction perfectly, which made a fraudulent request look processed and official. AI cannot tell you whether a letter is genuine.

Rule

Bank detail changes are verified by a phone call to a number already on file — never from the email. AI has no role in this step at all.

PROMPT 1 — PROFILE BEFORE YOU ANALYSE
The attached file is a SYNTHETIC purchase register of an Indian construction company (training data). Before any analysis, tell me: - number of rows and columns, and the date range of Posting_Date - number of entries and total Taxable_Value per Project_Code - total CGST, SGST and IGST for the whole file Show how you calculated each total. PROMPT 2 — THE EIGHT-POINT AUDIT CHECKLIST
Now audit every row against these checks. Use a tolerance of ₹1 for rounding. 1. Exact duplicates: same Vendor_Code, Invoice_No and Invoice_Total. 2. Near-duplicates: same Vendor_Code and Taxable_Value, where Invoice_No matches after removing "/", "-", spaces, "26-27" and leading zeros. 3. Qty × Rate does not equal Taxable_Value. 4. Taxable_Value + CGST + SGST + IGST does not equal Invoice_Total. 5. Tax type: if Vendor_State_Code differs from Place_of_Supply_State it should be IGST only; if they are the same it should be CGST + SGST only. 6. Vendor_GSTIN: must be 15 characters, first two digits must equal Vendor_State_Code, 14th character must be "Z". 7. Goods without GRN: HSN_SAC not starting with "99" and GRN_No blank. 8. Invoice_Date later than Posting_Date. Output one table: Entry_ID, check number, the values that prove it, suggested action. Then a summary: count of rows failing each check, and the ₹ value involved.
PROMPT 3 — THE CHALLENGER
For checks 2 and 5, list every Entry_ID you flagged and the exact values you compared. Now re-check the whole file for those two checks only. Did you miss any? Are any of your flags false positives? Be specific — if you change your answer, say which row and why.
PROMPT 4 — THE NOTE UPWARD
Draft a note of no more than 150 words to the Head of Accounts: findings by check, count and ₹ value, and the three actions to take before the auditors arrive. State facts only. Do not speculate about who made the errors or why. Add [confirm] against any figure I should re-check in Excel before sending.

✅ Prove it — before you score it

  • ▸ Arithmetic: pick one flagged row. Qty × Rate on your calculator. Does it match Taxable_Value?
  • ▸ Duplicate: filter the file (Sheets / Excel) on the Invoice_No the AI named. Do two rows really appear?
  • ▸ Tax type: look at Vendor_State_Code and Place_of_Supply_State in one flagged row. Which tax should apply?
