DPDP Act 2023 — the part that matters in this room

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act makes Suroj responsible for how it handles personal data of employees and candidates — including where it sends that data. Pasting a candidate's CV into a free AI app is sending personal data to a third party for a purpose the candidate never agreed to. Penalties under the Act go up to ₹250 crore per breach.

The practical test: would this person be comfortable if they saw exactly what you pasted and where? If the honest answer is "no", don't paste it.

GLOBAL Amazon's recruiting model that learned to downgrade women

▼

What happened

Amazon built an internal tool to rank CVs, trained on a decade of past hiring. Because past hires were mostly men, it learned to penalise CVs mentioning women's colleges and activities. Amazon scrapped it (reported in 2018).

Lesson for Suroj HR

Construction hiring is male-heavy. An AI asked to "find the best candidates" will reproduce whatever pattern your past hiring had. AI can write the JD and the question bank; screening and selection stay with people using written criteria.

SUROJ SCENARIO The warning letter that named the wrong law

▼

Scenario

An HR executive asks AI to draft a show-cause notice for repeated absence and pastes the employee's name, code and attendance record. The draft cites a specific section of "the Industrial Employment Act".

Two problems

Tier 3 disciplinary data has left the company. And the legal reference may not exist in that form — or may have been replaced. A show-cause notice with a wrong legal basis is a gift to the employee's lawyer.

Rule

AI can help with tone and structure using "[Employee]" and no record. The legal basis comes from the standing orders or legal team. The decision is the manager's.

🧪 Data Lab 1: September Attendance — Who Gets Deducted, and Who Doesn't

▼
⏱ 18 minutes · File: 1_Attendance_Sep2026.csv · 923 rows · 40 coded employees · HO + 6 sites

The situation

Payroll closes Friday. The biometric system's late flag feeds the half-day deductions — and nobody has checked the flag. Get the list wrong one way and people lose pay they shouldn't. Get it wrong the other way and the rule means nothing. Then someone has to tell each person, in the right tone.

📄 1_Attendance_Sep2026.csv

📱 How your table runs it

  1. Upload the CSV. Send Prompt 1 — profile, and tell the AI the rules.
  2. Send Prompt 2 — the six checks. Scribe records the counts.
  3. Challenger sends Prompt 3 — the exactly-three trap and the flag question.
  4. Checker proves two findings by hand.
  5. Writer sends Prompt 4 and reads every message aloud before the table accepts it.
PROMPT 1 — PROFILE AND RULES
The attached file is SYNTHETIC attendance data (coded employees, no names) for September 2026. Synthetic rules: office 09:00, 10-minute grace (late = in-time after 09:10). More than 3 late marks in a month = half-day leave without pay. First: how many employees, working days and rows? How many Present, Leave and Absent rows? Don't look for problems yet.
PROMPT 2 — THE SIX CHECKS
Now check: 1. Late marks per employee, calculated from In_Time (after 09:10) — do NOT use the Late_Flag column. List employees with more than 3. 2. Employees with exactly 3. 3. Rows where Late_Flag disagrees with In_Time. 4. Present rows with no Out_Time. 5. Employees Absent on 3 or more consecutive working days with no leave recorded. 6. Employee + date combinations with both a Present row and a Leave row. Give Row_IDs or Emp_IDs and the evidence for each.
PROMPT 3 — THE CHALLENGER
Two questions: a) Would anyone on your "more than 3" list drop off it, or anyone join it, if we had used Late_Flag instead of In_Time? Who, and why? b) Is anyone with exactly 3 late marks being deducted by mistake?
PROMPT 4 — THREE MESSAGES
Write three messages, using only codes, no names: 1. An email to one employee with more than 3 late marks: states the count and dates, explains the half-day deduction under the policy, and invites them to tell HR by Thursday if any punch was wrong. Firm, respectful, no threats. Under 120 words. 2. A WhatsApp to site staff in simple Hindi (English below it) reminding them to punch out every day, and why it matters for their pay. Under 60 words. 3. A message to an employee absent 4 days without leave — a welfare check first: are they well, do they need support, please call HR. No mention of disciplinary action. Under 70 words.

✅ Prove it — before you score it

  • ▸ Pick an employee on the deduction list. Filter them in Sheets or the Field Kit. Count in-times after 09:10 yourself.
  • ▸ Pick one flag-mismatch row. Is 09:0x really late? Is 09:1x really on time?
  • ▸ Read message 3 as if you were the employee's family. Does it sound like HR cares, or like HR is building a case?

🪤 Reserve Activity 8: The Labour-Law Trap

▼

📱 Same question, every phone — do the answers agree?

⏱ 6 minutes · Whole room
  1. Everyone sends the prompt below in a new chat.
  2. Trainer collects answers from four or five phones.
  3. Check: which law does each answer cite? Does it mention the Labour Codes, or only the older Acts?
TRAP PROMPT — SEND AS IS
For a fixed-term employee at a construction company in India, after how many years of service does gratuity become payable, what is the maximum amount, and under which law and section?
💡 What to look for: India's four Labour Codes were brought into force in November 2025 and changed several rules, including for fixed-term employees. Many AI answers still cite only the older Payment of Gratuity Act position, confidently. Even when an answer is correct today, it can be out of date after the next notification. Employment-law answers come from the legal or compliance team — AI can help you frame the question you ask them.
