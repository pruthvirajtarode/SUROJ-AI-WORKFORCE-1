1️⃣ What AI Is — and How It Differs from Normal Software (7 min)

▼
🎯 What we are trying to achieve

Feel the difference between rules a person writes and patterns a machine learns.

📦 What happens

Everyone votes on five cards; the last card shows the best systems mix both.

Normal software: rules written by people

Tally calculates GST because a programmer wrote the rule. Excel computes a total because someone wrote SUM. Same input, same output, every time — and it can only do what someone thought of.

Input → rules a person wrote → output

AI: rules learned from examples

Nobody writes the rule for “is this email spam?”. The system is shown millions of examples and learns the pattern itself. It can handle cases nobody anticipated — but it can also be wrong, and it can't always tell you why.

Examples → learned pattern → best guess

Words people use loosely — from widest to narrowest:

Artificial Intelligence
machines doing tasks that need judgement
⊃
Machine learning
learns patterns from data
⊃
Deep learning
very large “neural networks”
⊃
Generative AI
creates text, images, code
⊃
LLMs
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot

Three families of AI you already meet

FamilyWhat it doesEveryday exampleAt Suroj

GenerativeCreates new contentChatGPT writing an email; phone photo “magic eraser”Drafting an RFI, a pre-bid query, a monthly note

🗳️ 60-second room check — rule or learned?

GST = 18% of taxable value
Rule — a person wrote it once; it never changes on its own.
Your phone recognising your face
Learned — trained on many images; nobody wrote “if nose then…”.
Steel unit weight = d² ÷ 162
Rule — physics, written into the BBS sheet.
ChatGPT replying to a client email
Learned — patterns of language, not rules.
Spotting which invoices look like duplicates of each other
Can be either! Exact duplicates are a rule; “looks like a duplicate” is learned. Good AI work combines both.

2️⃣ How an LLM Works — What Happens in the Back End (12 min)

▼
🎯 What we are trying to achieve

See, not just hear, that an LLM picks the next word by probability — and why answers vary.

📦 What happens

Watch the bars change with the temperature slider; type a sentence and see the tokens.

1 · Pre-training
reads a huge slice of the internet, books, code — learns to predict the next word
→
2 · Fine-tuning & feedback
people rate answers; it learns to be helpful, follow instructions, refuse harm
→
3 · Your chat (inference)
your words → tokens → predict the next token, again and again

1 · Pre-training — reading at enormous scale

The model reads trillions of words. At every position it guesses the next word, checks the real one, and nudges billions of internal numbers (“parameters”) to guess better next time. This takes months on thousands of specialised chips. What comes out is not a database of facts — it's a compressed sense of how language, facts and reasoning usually fit together.

2 · Fine-tuning — learning to be an assistant

A raw model just continues text. To make it answer questions, follow instructions and decline harmful requests, it is trained further on example conversations, and people rate its answers so it learns which ones are better. That's why ChatGPT and Claude sound like assistants and not like autocomplete.

3 · What happens when you press Enter

  1. Your text is split into tokens — chunks of words.
  2. The model looks at all the tokens together (this is the “transformer” — it weighs which words matter to which).
  3. It produces a probability for every possible next token.
  4. It picks one (usually a likely one, with a little randomness), adds it, and repeats — token by token — until the answer is done.

Because it predicts, it…

BehaviourWhyWhat you do about it
Writes fluently in any styleIt has seen every styleTell it the tone, length and audience
Sounds confident when wrong (“hallucination”)A plausible next word isn't a checked factAsk for sources / clause / row; verify
Gives a different answer each timeThere's randomness in the pickingAsk for structure; regenerate and compare
Doesn't know your company, your tender, your budgetIt only knows what it read in training + what you give itAttach the document; give context
Doesn't know recent eventsTraining stops at a date (“knowledge cutoff”)Use web search mode for current facts
Gets long arithmetic wrong in its headDigits are predicted like wordsAsk it to calculate with code, or for the formula
Forgets things in a very long chat or huge fileIt can only hold so much at once (“context window”)Split the work; start a new chat per task

So how can it do real tasks?

Modern apps add tools around the model: reading uploaded files and images, searching the web, running code to calculate exactly, creating documents. When ChatGPT or Claude “analyses your spreadsheet”, the model is writing code and a computer runs it. That's why asking it to calculate beats asking it to guess.

“Is it thinking?”

Newer “reasoning” models write out intermediate steps before answering, which makes them much better at maths, logic and multi-step problems — and slower. It's still prediction underneath, just with a scratchpad. It has no beliefs, no stake in being right, and no accountability. You do.

THE TEMPLATE — SAVE IT IN YOUR PHONE NOTES

Task: [one clear job] Context: [what the attached file is / the situation / the date] Format: [table / bullets / email; length; language] Constraints: Use only what I give you. Don't invent facts. Five habits that matter more than clever wording

  • Give it the material — it can't read your mind or your files unless you attach them
  • One job per prompt — then follow up
  • Talk back — “shorter”, “in Hindi”, “as a table”, “what did you miss?”
  • Let it ask — “Ask me 3 questions before you start”
  • Check two things before you trust the rest

5️⃣ Generative AI vs Agentic AI (6 min)

▼

Generative AI — you ask, it creates

You give a prompt; it gives back text, a table, an image, code. Every step after that is yours: you check it, you send it, you act.

Agentic AI — you give a goal, it takes steps

It plans, uses tools (opens files, searches, updates a sheet, sends a draft), checks its own work and reports back — within limits you set. It acts in the world, so it needs permissions and approvals.

A spectrum, not a switch:

Chat
answers questions
→
Chat + tools
reads files, searches, calculates
→
Automation
fixed steps someone designed
→
Agent
decides the steps itself

▼

🗳️ Use it, check it, or don't — vote, then reveal

Summarise a 40-page document into one page
Excellent at it — but check it didn't drop the one clause that matters.
Translate a site notice into Hindi and Marathi
Strong — have a native speaker glance at it before printing.
Draft a polite reminder email
Ideal job. You still press Send.
Total a 2,000-row cost sheet in its head
Ask for code or a formula instead.
Tell you the latest GST rate change
Its knowledge stops at a date — use search mode and confirm on the official source.
Decide whether to bid on a tender
A business decision with accountability. AI can prepare the facts; people decide.
Turn messy notes into a table
One of its best uses.
Count sleeves on a photographed drawing

Write an Excel formula
Fast — test it on three rows you know.
Decide which worker to dismiss
Decisions about people need human judgement, fairness and accountability.
Explain an unfamiliar term (e.g. CPI, EMD)
A patient tutor. Ask for an example from construction.
Cite a court case or IS code clause for a claim
Classic hallucination risk — open the source before using it.

When confident text went out unchecked

  • Lawyers, New York, 2023 (Mata v. Avianca): filed a brief citing court cases ChatGPT had invented. The court sanctioned them.
  • Air Canada, 2024: its website chatbot told a customer about a refund policy that didn't exist. A tribunal held the airline responsible for what its chatbot said.
  • Deloitte Australia, 2025: a report for a government department was found to contain references that didn't exist; the firm agreed to refund part of its fee.

The common thread: the AI wasn't punished. The people and companies who sent it were.

7️⃣ Safe and Responsible Use (6 min)

▼

What may go into an AI tool

TierExamplesFree personal app?
PUBLICGeneral questions, published standards, training data like today'sYes
INTERNALYour own notes with names removed, templates, general process descriptionsOnly anonymised
CONFIDENTIALClient documents and drawings, prices, budgets, margins, contracts, salariesNo — company-approved accounts only
NEVERPasswords, OTPs, bank details, Aadhaar/PAN, firewall or system configurationsNever, in any AI tool

What happened elsewhere

  • Samsung, 2023: engineers pasted internal source code into ChatGPT to debug it; the company then restricted generative AI use.
  • Arup, 2024: an employee in Hong Kong joined a video call with deepfaked senior colleagues and transferred about US$25 million to fraudsters.

AI also makes fraud more convincing: verify any payment or password request by calling back on a number you already have.

Personal data

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 puts obligations on how organisations handle people's personal data. Workers' names, phone numbers, Aadhaar, salaries and attendance are personal data — they don't go into personal AI apps.

Ten rules for the card on your desk

  1. Use the company-approved account for company work.
  2. Turn off “improve the model” / training on personal accounts.
  3. Never paste passwords, OTPs, bank or ID numbers.
  4. Remove names and figures before using a personal app.
  5. Ask for sources — and open them.
  6. Check numbers by calculation, not by trust.
  7. AI drafts; you decide and sign.
  8. No decisions about people by AI alone.
  9. Be careful with others' copyrighted material and drawings.
  10. Unsure? Ask EDP before you paste.

The situation

Three columns on a BBS are arithmetic: total length = bars × cut length; unit weight = d² ÷ 162; weight = length × unit weight. Each can be typed wrong, and the steel order and the subcontractor bill both follow the total.

🪜 Steps to get it done

  1. Download or preview the files above (buttons in the blue box).
  2. Attach the BBS and send Prompt 1 — check every row.
  3. Checker recomputes one row: d² ÷ 162 and number × length.
  4. Send Prompt 2 — Excel check formulas; test them on one row.
PROMPT 1 — CHECK EVERY ROW
For every row of the attached SYNTHETIC BBS, recompute: No_of_Bars × Cut_Length_m; Dia_mm² ÷ 162; and the weight. List rows where Total_Length_m (±0.05), Unit_Wt_kg_per_m (±0.005) or Weight_kg (±0.5) is wrong, with typed and correct values. Give the total weight as typed and as recomputed, and by diameter. Use code.
PROMPT 2 — MAKE IT REUSABLE
Write Excel formulas for three check columns on this layout (columns D = Dia, E = Nos, F = Cut length, G = Total length, H = Unit wt, I = Weight) that show OK or the correct value. Give one test row with the expected result.
